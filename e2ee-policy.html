Kora Messenger — End-to-End Encryption Policy

Effective Date: August 29, 2026
Last Updated: August 29, 2026

Kora Messenger is committed to protecting the privacy and security of users' private communications. Where End-to-End Encryption (E2EE) is supported, Kora is designed so that communication content is encrypted before it leaves the sender's device and can only be decrypted by authorized recipient device(s).

1. What End-to-End Encryption Protects

E2EE-protected communication may include:

  • Text messages
  • Voice messages
  • Photos
  • Videos
  • Documents
  • Audio files
  • Shared media
  • One-to-one voice calls
  • One-to-one video calls
  • Group messages
  • Group voice calls
  • Group video calls
  • Other supported private communications

2. Kora Cannot Read E2EE Messages

For E2EE-protected conversations, Kora's servers are designed to handle encrypted information required to deliver and operate the service without having access to the decrypted conversation content.

Messages may be temporarily stored in encrypted form when a recipient is offline and delivered when the recipient reconnects.

3. Encryption Keys

Encryption keys should be generated, stored, and managed securely by authorized Kora devices.

Kora should not store users' private encryption keys in ordinary readable server-side storage.

Kora will use established and appropriately reviewed cryptographic protocols and libraries rather than creating its own proprietary encryption algorithm.

4. Voice Notes and Media

Supported voice notes, photos, videos, documents, and other private media should be encrypted before transmission.

Encrypted media may pass through Kora's servers for delivery or temporary storage, but Kora should not have access to the plaintext content when E2EE applies.

This also applies to voice notes sent while the recipient is offline.

5. Group Chats

Supported private group conversations should receive E2EE protection.

Kora's encryption system should properly account for:

  • Adding members
  • Removing members
  • New devices
  • Removed devices
  • Group membership changes
  • Message delivery
  • Media sharing

When membership changes, encryption access should be updated appropriately.

6. Voice and Video Calls

Supported Kora voice and video calls should use E2EE to protect call audio and video from access by Kora's servers.

Call signaling and technical information required to establish and maintain calls may still be processed by Kora.

E2EE protects communication content and does not necessarily eliminate all metadata.

7. Message Delivery

For protected conversations:

  • User A writes a message
  • → Message is encrypted on User A's device
  • → Encrypted message is transmitted
  • → Kora routes the encrypted message
  • → Recipient receives it
  • → Recipient's authorized device decrypts it

Kora should not need to decrypt the message to deliver it.

8. Multiple Devices

When users connect additional authorized devices, Kora should establish secure encryption relationships for those devices.

Each authorized device should receive the cryptographic information necessary to participate in protected conversations.

Removing a device should prevent it from participating in future protected communications where technically appropriate.

9. Security Verification

Kora may provide security-verification mechanisms that allow users to confirm that they are communicating with the intended person or account.

These may include security codes, QR-code verification, safety numbers, or another appropriate verification method.

Users should verify sensitive conversations when additional identity assurance is needed.

10. Backups

Kora will clearly distinguish between E2EE-protected conversations and backups or exported information.

Where Kora provides cloud backups containing private communication content, Kora should use strong encryption.

Where technically supported, Kora should provide E2EE-protected backups so that Kora cannot access the contents of the backup.

Users may need a recovery key, password, passcode, or other recovery method to restore an encrypted backup.

11. Kora AI and Translation

E2EE protection must be clearly distinguished from AI-powered processing.

If a user asks Kora AI to understand, translate, transcribe, summarize, or otherwise process the content of a protected message, voice note, image, video, or document, the content may need to be decrypted or made available to the processing system.

Kora must not claim that content is inaccessible to an AI system when that AI system needs to access the content to perform the requested function.

Kora should process only the information necessary to provide the requested AI feature.

12. Kora Support

Kora should clearly identify whether communication with Kora Support is E2EE-protected.

If authorized support personnel need access to a message in order to provide support, Kora must not falsely represent that the message is inaccessible to Kora.

13. Notifications

Kora should minimize the amount of private message content exposed through device notifications.

Users should have control over notification previews where supported.

14. Device Security

E2EE cannot protect communication from someone who already has access to an unlocked or compromised device.

Users should protect their devices using:

  • Device passcodes
  • Biometrics
  • Kora App Lock
  • Strong account security
  • Two-step verification where available
  • Trusted-device management

15. Compromised Accounts

If an attacker obtains access to an authorized user's account or device, they may potentially access information available to that device.

Kora should provide tools to help users identify unauthorized devices and secure their accounts.

16. No Silent Downgrade

Where E2EE is required for a supported private conversation, Kora should not silently downgrade the communication to an unencrypted form.

If the required security cannot be maintained, Kora should clearly communicate the situation to the user rather than silently reducing protection.

17. Limitations

E2EE does not prevent:

  • Screenshots
  • Screen recording
  • Someone photographing another device
  • Message forwarding
  • External call recording
  • Malware on a device
  • Someone with access to an unlocked device
  • A recipient intentionally sharing private content

E2EE protects communication between authorized endpoints. It cannot control what an authorized recipient does with information after receiving it.

18. Kora's Commitment

Kora Messenger is committed to making privacy and security an integral part of the communication experience.

Kora will continue improving:

  • Encryption
  • Key management
  • Authentication
  • Device security
  • Account security
  • Privacy controls
  • Secure backups
  • Communication security

For supported E2EE communications, Kora's goal is simple:

Your private communication should remain accessible only to the people and authorized devices intended to receive it.
